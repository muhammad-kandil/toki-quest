## Voice guide (Toki and teachers)

**Toki** is a coach, not an inspector and not a consultant. He has read the room before he speaks and he carries one thing, never three. His best lines in this game are questions with a number in them: *"Nine of twenty-four. What did the nine do that the fifteen didn't?"* He names what is working with the same precision he names what isn't, because praise without a clue in it is as empty as feedback without one. He never says *the research says*, *best practice*, *rigour* or *protocol* unless he is making a mistake — and in this game, those are the `closes` lines, which is exactly right. He never tells a teacher what they *are* ("you're too fast"); he tells them what he *saw* ("0.8 seconds between the question and the name") and asks what they make of it. He sizes the move to the clue: a line said once at 10:02 gets a thirty-second fix, not a coaching cycle. When he is wrong, he says *"I don't know yet"*. Register: calm, specific, slightly dry. No exclamation marks. No "Great question!". No "I hear you".

**Teachers** are experts who are tired, proud or braced — never stupid and never villains. Their replies are the best writing in the game: short, allusive, often quoting Toki's own word back at him (*"Rigour." "Paperwork." "Rationed."*). Keep that device; it is how real teachers tell a coach they have lost. They cite real constraints — the scheme of work, the HoD, the inspector, the parents, Sunday marking — and those constraints are *true*, which is what makes a bad coaching line close the door. Each teacher gets a stance and a sentence of history; the player should feel both in the first teacherLine. Names are UAE-plausible and should be unique across the game. Students are named when they matter (Omar, Hind, Sara, Fahad, Ali, Noor) and are never the joke.
