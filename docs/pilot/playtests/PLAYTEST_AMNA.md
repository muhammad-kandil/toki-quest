# Toki's Coaching Quest — playtest diary (Amna)

**Who I am:** senior DLIS, Abu Dhabi. 12 years teaching, 5 coaching. Played on my phone (390×844), English, in the gaps between two school visits and a car ride. First time seeing the game. Nobody briefed me.

**What I played:** World 1 (all five classrooms + The Perfect Classroom), then World 7 (The Lethal Mutation Academy, four classrooms + The Invisible Mutation) after the tester unlocked it for me. Times below are my tapping/reading time per room, roughly; add a minute or so if you read slower than I do.

Screenshots are in this folder (00_title.png … 96_customize.png).

---

## First impression (title → map)

Title screen is clean. The line "A judgement game, not a quiz. Nothing is timed, nobody ranks you — and the teacher answers back" is exactly the right promise for someone like me, and I noted it so I could hold the game to it.

"Start Your First Visit" drops me straight onto a winding map of eight worlds. No tutorial, which I appreciated — but I tapped World 1 and nothing happened for a good three seconds, so I tapped again. Every screen transition in this game has that lag. On a phone, between visits, three seconds of nothing reads as "it's broken".

The map is pretty. The world names are fine. "The Master DLIS" as World 8 made me smile; by the end of World 1 the game had already given me a badge called "Master DLIS", which rather undercuts it.

---

## World 1 — The Busy Classroom

The world screen lists the doors: *The Talkative Classroom, The Activity Overload, The Output Illusion, The Assessment Trap, The Gamification Mutation.* Before I've opened a single door I know what every drift is. That's a design problem, and it got worse inside.

### Classroom 1 — The Talkative Classroom (English) — ~4 min, most of it arguing with the game

**The scene.** Above the title, in teal caps: **PARTICIPATION ≠ LEARNING.** So. The three evidence hotspots are large and easy to hit. Clue cards: group table ("I think it's good." "Me too." Nobody asks why), the board ("Discuss the chapter" — no question, no claim), the teacher ("Great energy, everyone!" — not one follow-up question). The cards replace each other, so only the last one stays on screen; I found them again later under "Evidence notes", which is good, but I'd rather they stacked on the scene.

**What I thought the drift was.** Thinking quality. The structure for talk is there, the cognitive demand isn't. I picked **Thinking quality** — accepted first try. But there was no tick, no "right", nothing. The screen just slid on to "Assess the Risk". I genuinely wasn't sure whether I'd been marked right until the reveal.

**Risk — where it went wrong.** "How serious is this drift, right now?" I picked **High — actively undermining the lesson**, because a whole period of empty agreement is a wasted period. "Look again." A hint appeared half off the bottom of the screen: *"how much of what you saw would still be there tomorrow if nothing changed — and how much of it is already working?"* with a pointer to the teacher clue. That hint is a riddle, not a hint; it can be read as "this will persist" (so, more serious) or "lots is working" (so, less). I tried **Moderate** — wrong. And at that point the red mark on "High" had vanished, so I lost track of what I'd already tried. I tried **Lethal Mutation** — wrong. **Minor** was the only thing left. The reveal then said MINOR DRIFT and explained the drift nicely — but never said one word about *why* this is minor rather than high. I lost 21 points and a badge on a judgement call the game didn't bother to defend. This is the one moment in the whole session I felt talked down to, and it's because the game was certain and silent at the same time.

**Coaching move.** **Help the teacher add prompts requiring evidence and justification** — right first time. Bonus "why" question — right first time. Both were easy; the wrong options were obviously wrong.

**The conversation.** This is the good part. "Toki's read · Ms. Mariam, proud. Two years ago this room was silent rows. She built this noise on purpose." Her first line — "Did you see them? Every single group was talking. Last year I couldn't get three hands up." — is a real teacher. I chose *"I did — the table by the door didn't stop once. What did you hear them saying to each other?"* and she came back with *"Honestly? A lot of 'yes, I agree'. I noticed that too, actually."* Then: *"So what, I make them stop agreeing with each other?"* — a little defensive, exactly right. I picked the "convince the other table" reframe and she said she'd try it Thursday. "PRACTICE RESTORED". I felt the consequence, mostly because the reveal's "what happened next" line ("the sentences now start with 'because'") paid it off.

One UI thing: in that conversation the third option (the blunt "lacked academic rigour" one) had a teal highlighted border before I'd touched anything. It looked recommended. I think it's a hover state from where my thumb was, but on a phone it reads as a hint toward the wrong answer.

### Classroom 2 — The Activity Overload (Science) — ~1.5 min

**BUSY ≠ LEARNING** over the door. The clue cards don't just show evidence, they annotate it: *"The objective describes activity, not learning."* *"No column for 'understood it.'"* I'm not spotting anything here; I'm being read to.

Drift: **Completing the activities** — first try. Risk: the objective literally says "complete all 5 stations" and the tracker rewards finishing, so I said **Lethal Mutation — the practice has lost its purpose**. Wrong. Hint: *"Is the missing column a crack, or has the whole floor gone?"* — so, lower. **Moderate** — accepted. Again no reason given at the reveal for why Moderate. The game told me the objective was wrong, then told me that was a crack.

Move: **Reconnect every activity to a clear learning objective** — first try. Why: first try.

Conversation with Mr. Yousef, "eager", who plans rotations until midnight. I asked what station three was for; he said *"Station three… density, I think. Or was that four. Let me check the plan."* Then *"…Two of them. Maybe. The others are more 'do', less 'explain'. I see what you're asking."* That is precisely how that conversation goes in real life. "What happened next": four stations, each ending with a sentence the student says out loud, "he dropped the two he couldn't explain himself." Best line in the world.

### Classroom 3 — The Output Illusion (Art) — ~1 min

**PRODUCING ≠ UNDERSTANDING.** Drift: **Demonstration of understanding** — first try. Risk: **High** — first try (so it's not always low; I just can't see the rule). Move: I hesitated between the rubric fix (the evidence pointed straight at the rubric) and **Embed explanation, reasoning, and application into the task** — picked the latter because a copied textbook caption is scientifically accurate and still proves nothing. Right. Why: first try.

Conversation with Ms. Fatima — "The wall is her reputation in this school. Whatever Toki says about it, she will hear as being said about her." That read is better than most coaching PD I've sat through. Two of the three options in turn one were genuinely good coaching lines (ask her to predict what Sara would say; ask to hear one student explain without reading) — the first time a choice felt like a real decision rather than spot-the-non-insulting-option. The QR-code idea landed well and she said so. Badge: "Coaching Specialist" — after one clean room. Bit grand.

### Classroom 4 — The Assessment Trap (Math) — ~1 min

**SCORES ≠ LEARNING.** Twelve weeks of colour-coded scores, same students at the bottom in week 3 and week 11, planner unchanged since September. Drift: **Instructional response to the data** — first try. Risk: I braced for another "Look again" and said **Lethal Mutation** — accepted. So 12 weeks of unused data is lethal, but "discuss the chapter" with no question is minor. Fine. I'd just like to be told the rule once.

Conversation with Mr. Khalid, 4:10 pm, "tired", "buried under it. Anything that sounds like 'more' will not land." I asked about Question 4 last Tuesday, eleven wrong the same way, what happened Wednesday. *"Wednesday was… the next unit. I didn't go back. I don't have time to go back."* Then the pushback: *"You want me to reteach every time eleven kids get something wrong? I'd never finish the syllabus."* I offered dropping one quiz to fund five minutes on Wednesday. *"Drop a quiz. And just do the one question. …Nobody has ever suggested I do less."* I'm stealing that line for my next visit. Two badges popped at once here (Purpose Protector, Mutation Hunter) — badge inflation.

### Classroom 5 — The Gamification Mutation (any subject) — ~1 min

The door is called "Mutation". So the risk answer is on the door. **Speed and performance** — first try. **Lethal Mutation** — first try, because the title told me. Move: **Reward reasoning, explanation, reflection, and improvement — not speed alone**. Why: first try.

Conversation with Ms. Noura, "the teacher students ask for. Take the games away and you take her away." I asked who got it right in round two but didn't buzz first. *"Hind. Hind had it. She always has it, she's just slow to press. She was fourth."* Explanation round, one in three. *"The tables would go quiet for it… and then loud again."* Good. Clean 125-point run — no badge this time, though the reveal text promised one for "a clean first-try run and a teacher who changed something". Inconsistent.

### The Final Visit — The Perfect Classroom — ~2.5 min, and the weakest thing in World 1

Framed as an audit, six dimensions, and for each: "Your call: does this dimension check out?" with two buttons. There is **no evidence on the screen**. Dimension 1 says only "Toki reads the objectives posted on the board and checks them against the lesson." Checks them against what? I'm asked to judge a thing I haven't been shown. I guessed "It checks out" from the intro paragraph (which said the objectives were crystal clear). Right. Dimension 2, The Tasks — nothing to go on; after five rooms where the drift lived in the task I said "The drift is here". Wrong, and the hint was *"Check the tasks against the objectives you just confirmed — do they actually connect?"* — I was never shown the tasks. That's a coin flip with a penalty attached. Dimensions 3, 4, 5: guessed "checks out" from the intro's adjectives. Dimension 6 opens with *"Toki does something different now"* — flashing neon sign — and the drift is there.

Then "Locate the Mutation" asks where the problem lives, one screen after a paragraph told me it's in what the students have learned to do. Then "The Hardest Coaching Move" — and the correct option is the only one that isn't a cliché. **And then it ends.** No conversation. The one room where the coaching really is hard, the teacher never answers back. The closing paragraph ("You may have noticed — this game never rewarded you for being fast…") is a bit pleased with itself. The idea underneath — students who have learned to perform learning — is genuinely the right idea for a final visit; it's just not played, it's narrated.

World complete, badges 3 → 4 with no fanfare, no "next world" nudge, just the same list of doors with ticks.

**World 1 — worth my time: 3/5.** Conversations 5/5, scenarios 4/5, the identify/move/why steps 2/5 (too easy, answers printed above the door), the risk step 1/5 (opaque), the Perfect Classroom 2/5.

## World 7 — The Lethal Mutation Academy

Different mechanic, and it's the mechanic the whole game should use.

### Classroom 1 — The Almost Perfect Classroom (Maths) — ~1.5 min

**IMPLEMENTATION ≠ IMPACT.** Four hotspots. Then "File the Diagnosis: tick every finding the evidence supports — and nothing it doesn't," plus a "No drift here — file it" button. Finally a step where I'm doing the job.

Evidence: objective referred to twice; four groups with leaders, every sheet finished; exit ticket 9 of 24 correct and "thanks for a great lesson"; an evaluation form with every box ticked and no box for what students can now do. I ticked **The lesson is judged by what the teacher did, not by what students learned** and nothing else — "the objective is decorative" is a trap because it was used twice. On filing, every finding got its own verdict: *"Healthy — It was referred to twice and the exit ticket tested it. The objective is alive."* and the line *"Everything else was healthy — and saying so is part of the diagnosis."* That is how I think about an observation. This is what World 1 lacked.

Risk: in a world called the Lethal Mutation Academy, with a model classroom where 15 of 24 can't do the objective, I said **Lethal Mutation**. Wrong. The hint — *"If this room is the one new teachers are sent to watch, what spreads with it?"* — argues for *more* severity, not less. **High** was accepted. I still cannot tell you what separates High from Lethal in this game, and the reveal still doesn't say.

Move: **"What can they do now that they couldn't at 8:00?"** Conversation with Mr. Saeed, "told for three years that this is the model classroom." I asked the question nobody asks; *"…Nine of twenty-four on the exit ticket. I saw it. I've been thinking about it since."* Then *"But the lesson was good — you saw it. So what was it?"* I said I didn't know yet and asked what the nine did that the fifteen didn't. *"They… were the ones who wrote. The rest watched the leader write. The roles. The roles decide who thinks."* He got there himself. 19 of 24 on Thursday. Excellent.

### Classroom 2 — The Quiet Lesson (English) — ~1 min

**UNIMPRESSIVE ≠ DRIFTING** — the door gives it away again, but honestly the evidence would have too: twelve minutes of live modelled writing, 26 whiteboards up three times, margin notes that changed the next day's paragraph, the Alef report used as a diagnostic to decide who she visits first. A visiting leader had written "low engagement, no technology". I filed **No drift here**. Every finding came back "Healthy" with a reason. Risk: **No drift** — accepted. Move: name precisely what is working and correct the note.

Conversation with Ms. Hessa, "defensive… marked down for it before." I told her I'd counted the whiteboards. *"…You counted the whiteboards. Nobody has ever counted the whiteboards."* Then *"And when the next observer walks in and sees a quiet room?"* The third option — put the objective on the board and open the Alef dashboard, "it ticks the boxes" — is what half my colleagues would actually say, and the game is right that it's the wrong answer. I offered to bring the next observer and hand them a whiteboard. This room is the best-written thing in the game and the one I'd put in front of every school leader who writes "low engagement" on a form.

Oddities: the outcome stamp says "PRACTICE RESTORED" on a lesson where nothing needed restoring, and I earned the badge *"Mutation Hunter — You can name a lethal mutation when you see one"* for correctly saying there wasn't one.

### Classroom 3 — The Showcase Lesson (Science) — ~1 min

**SEVERAL DRIFTS, ONE PRIORITY.** Announced observation, rehearsed. Eleven questions answered by the same three students; "Finished? Go on Alef" as a prize; two genuinely different task cards assigned from last week's diagnostic; a feelings-faces exit ticket. I ticked three — **three students doing the thinking**, **Alef as reward**, **exit ticket measures enjoyment** — and left "differentiation is cosmetic" and "the hook video wasted the lesson" alone (no evidence for the hook; the differentiation is real). All correct; the filing text — *"Three findings, two healthy practices protected, one priority chosen. That is a diagnosis, not a list."* — is the thesis of the whole game in one line. Risk **High** — first try. Move: start with the one closest to learning and let the rest wait a week.

Ms. Amal, "two years in, rehearsed for a week. She will take one thing. If Toki gives her three, she will take none." *"Was the hook too long? I thought the hook was too long."* I told her the hook was fine and asked who answered the eleven questions. *"…Those three. Every time. I didn't even — I was so focused on the timing."* Whiteboards before hands. *"That's… one change. I can do one change by Sunday."* Felt true. Felt like the consequence of choosing one thing instead of three.

### Classroom 4 — The Small Thing (Arabic) — ~1 min

**PROPORTION IS JUDGEMENT.** Strong lesson; success criteria read at 10:02 and never again; two students at the door can't say what they got better at. I ticked only **success criteria shared, then never revisited**. "Pacing is rushed" is bait ("ended on the bell with the work done. Nothing was cut.") and "teacher talk dominates" got the best healthy-verdict in the game: *"No evidence either way, which means no finding. A diagnosis needs evidence, not a feeling."* Risk **Minor** — first try. Move: a light touch, one closing question, leave the strong lesson alone.

Ms. Aisha, twelve years in, marking books: *"Go on, then. What's the programme this term?"* I said no programme, one thirty-second thing. *"Thirty seconds. Go on."* … *"'Which one did we meet.' …That's it? That's a question. I can ask a question."* This is the room I'd show a new DLIS who arrives with a coaching cycle in a binder.

Copy bug on the world screen afterwards: "The Final Visit awaits. When you're ready, enter **The Perfect Classroom**." — but the door here is "The Invisible Mutation".

### World Challenge — The Invisible Mutation — ~1.5 min

Five scenes, each with an actual paragraph of evidence before the call (this is what World 1's Perfect Classroom should have done). Grade 7 objective read back word for word — pattern. Grade 4 teacher abandons her plan for a misconception — healthy. Data meeting that ends with "push completion" every week — pattern. Grade 9 marking that breaks the policy and keeps the purpose — healthy. "Thumbs up if you understood", 26 thumbs, "100% understood" in the register — pattern. 5/5, and each one felt like a real judgement, especially the two rule-breakers.

Then "Name the Pattern" — the answer is the subtitle printed on the door ("PRACTICE KEPT, PURPOSE GONE"). Risk: Lethal Mutation (the only time I was confident). Systemic move: replace "is the practice present?" with "is it still doing what it was introduced to do?" in every form. Again no conversation at the end — the leadership team "asks for a recommendation" and I pick it from a list.

**World 7 — worth my time: 4/5.** Diagnosis mechanic 5/5, conversations 5/5, risk step still 2/5, boss 4/5. Would be 5/5 if the door subtitles stopped announcing the answer.

## UI friction (phone)

- **Transition lag.** ~3 s of nothing after tapping a map node or a door. I double-tapped twice before I learned to wait.
- **No exit from inside a classroom.** There's no back button once you're in a scene. If I'm interrupted, my only exits are reload (goes to the title, classroom progress lost — acceptable, rooms are short) or the browser/hardware Back — which **leaves the app entirely** (went to a blank page). On an Android phone that's the back button.
- **Hint cut off.** The "Look again" hint renders below the fold on the risk screen; you have to know to scroll.
- **Wrong-answer marks vanish.** After a second wrong pick on the risk step, the first wrong option loses its red styling. On a four-option list with two wrong already, I couldn't remember which ones I'd tried.
- **Phantom highlight.** A choice card can carry a teal "selected" border before you touch it (a hover state left where your thumb was). On a phone it reads as a recommendation.
- **Top-bar buttons are 36×36 px** (Back, sound, Arabic toggle). Below the comfortable 44 pt. Choice cards and hotspots are big and fine.
- **Reveal opens mid-scroll** — the "MINOR DRIFT" header was above the fold on first paint; I saw "WHY IT HAPPENED" first.
- **Clue cards replace each other** on the scene; you can't re-read the earlier ones without going forward to the "Evidence notes" drawer.
- **The disabled "0/3" pill** on the scene is styled like a button. I tapped it.
- **Arabic toggle works well.** One tap, full RTL flip, numbers and ticks render correctly, Back arrow mirrors. No complaints — one of the most solid things in the app.
- **"Reset Progress"** sits at the bottom of the cosmetics screen, with nothing between it and the skin tiles.
- **Score/badge messaging is inconsistent:** reveal says badges need "a clean first-try run and a teacher who changed something"; my clean run in Classroom 5 earned nothing, my first clean run earned one, Classroom 4 earned two. "Master DLIS" badge arrived after one world. Some reveals show a "Total", some don't.

## What felt unfair, patronising, or too easy

1. **The answer is written above every door.** PARTICIPATION ≠ LEARNING, BUSY ≠ LEARNING, SCORES ≠ LEARNING, UNIMPRESSIVE ≠ DRIFTING, PRACTICE KEPT, PURPOSE GONE — on the scene screen, before I've looked at a single piece of evidence. Plus door names like "The Assessment Trap" and "The Gamification Mutation" (which also hands you the risk answer). I'm never spotting the drift; I'm confirming the headline.
2. **The risk step is a guessing game with a penalty.** Five of my eleven rooms sent me "looking again" on severity, once four times. The hints are koans. The reveal never justifies the severity it wanted, only the drift. If Minor vs High is a judgement — and the game says it's a judgement game — then show me the reasoning when we disagree.
3. **World 1's Perfect Classroom asks for six calls with no evidence.** That isn't judgement, it's a coin and a penalty.
4. **Clue cards that do the analysis for me** ("The objective describes activity, not learning"). Give me the objective; let me notice.
5. Mildly: the closing "you may have noticed this game never rewarded you for being fast" paragraph. I noticed. You don't need to tell me you were clever.

What was *not* patronising: every single conversation. The teacher reads ("she will hear it as being said about her", "anything that sounds like 'more' will not land", "if Toki gives her three, she will take none") are the best coaching prompts I've read in a year, and the wrong options in the conversations are wrong in exactly the ways real DLISs get it wrong.

## Would I keep playing without being asked?

World 1: probably not past Classroom 3 — I'd have stopped after the four-tap risk fight in Classroom 1 if nobody was watching, because the game had just told me I was wrong four times and declined to say why. World 7: yes. I'd finish it on the drive home, and I'd replay The Quiet Lesson to screenshot Ms. Hessa for our next leadership meeting. If every world played like World 7 I'd recommend it in the DLIS WhatsApp group without being asked.

## What I'd tell a colleague

"Skip the quizzy bits, don't argue with the risk step, and read the conversations — they're the training. The quiet-lesson one will make you want to go and apologise to someone."

---

## 3 things to fix first

1. **Stop printing the answer above the door.** Remove the "X ≠ Y" kicker from the scene screen (keep it for the reveal), and rename or hide door titles until a room is completed. Then move World 7's "tick every finding + No drift" diagnosis mechanic into Worlds 1–6 so there's something to actually judge.
2. **Make the risk step defensible.** Either show the severity reasoning in the reveal every time ("Minor because: …"), or let the first risk answer stand with feedback instead of a "Look again" loop, or drop the step to three bands. Also keep wrong picks visibly marked and put the hint above the fold.
3. **Give the final visits evidence and a teacher.** World 1's Perfect Classroom needs a scene per dimension (like World 7's boss) and a closing conversation — the "hardest coaching move" is the one that is never actually practised. Add an in-classroom exit button and trap the hardware Back so it goes to the world screen, not out of the app.
